# Case Report NDH-008: Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma with nodal spread (MALIGNANT)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-38891 | 44-year-old male | Endocrine Surgery | 2025-07-08

## Summary
A 2.1 cm thyroid nodule that is truly papillary thyroid cancer, with spread to central neck lymph nodes. It shares the word "papillary" with NIFTP (NDH-007) but behaves very differently.

## Presentation
Firm neck lump noticed by patient. Normal thyroid function.

## Imaging
- Ultrasound: solid hypoechoic nodule, taller-than-wide, irregular margin, microcalcifications, 2.1 cm. TI-RADS 5.
- Abnormal level VI lymph nodes.

## Cytology and molecular
FNA: Bethesda VI (malignant), papillary carcinoma. BRAF V600E positive.

## Pathology
Total thyroidectomy with central neck dissection. Classic papillary carcinoma, 2.1 cm, minor extrathyroidal extension absent, lymphovascular invasion present. Central nodes: 3 of 9 positive.

## Final diagnosis and category
Papillary thyroid carcinoma, pT2 N1a M0, AJCC stage I (age under 55). Category: MALIGNANT TUMOR (cancer), although generally excellent prognosis.

## Plan
Radioactive iodine ablation based on intermediate risk. TSH suppression. Thyroglobulin and ultrasound follow-up.

## Why this is NOT NIFTP
True papillae, invasion, BRAF mutation and nodal metastases.
