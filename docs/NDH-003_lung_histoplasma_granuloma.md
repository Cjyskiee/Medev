# Case Report NDH-003: Histoplasma Granuloma (lung INFECTION that mimics cancer)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-47720 | 49-year-old male | Former river-valley farm worker | Pulmonology | 2025-08-19

## Summary
A 2.2 cm lung nodule with spiky edges and very high PET uptake, referred as "suspected lung cancer" and scheduled for lobectomy. Biopsy showed a fungal granuloma. This is not a tumor of any kind, neither benign nor malignant.

## Presentation
Mild night sweats, no weight loss. 15 pack-year smoker.

## Imaging
- Left lower lobe nodule, 2.2 cm, irregular spiculated margin.
- Two small satellite nodules, one with central calcification.
- PET-CT: SUV 5.4, strongly avid (inflammation also lights up PET).
- Size roughly stable over 5 months (2.1 to 2.2 cm).

## Pathology
Necrotizing granuloma. GMS stain shows narrow-based budding yeast. Urine Histoplasma antigen positive. No malignant cells.

## Final diagnosis and category
Histoplasma granuloma. Category: NON-NEOPLASTIC INFECTIOUS MIMIC (not a tumor, not cancer).

## Plan
Surgery cancelled. Infectious disease consult. Observation, because asymptomatic solitary granuloma usually needs no antifungal drug. Repeat CT at 6 months.

## Why this is NOT lung cancer or a benign tumor
Satellite calcified nodules, stable size, positive fungal antigen and necrotizing granuloma. High PET uptake and spiculation do not equal cancer.
