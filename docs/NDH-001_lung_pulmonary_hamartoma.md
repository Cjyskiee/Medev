# Case Report NDH-001: Pulmonary Hamartoma (BENIGN lung tumor)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-48213 | 58-year-old male | 30 pack-year smoker | Pulmonology | 2025-03-11

## Summary
A 1.8 cm well-defined lung nodule found by accident on a trauma CT. The patient is a heavy smoker, so lung cancer was suspected first. Biopsy proved it is a hamartoma, a benign tumor made of normal lung tissue types (cartilage, fat, airway lining) growing in a disorganized way.

## Presentation
No cough, weight loss or hemoptysis. Incidental finding.

## Imaging
- Right lower lobe nodule, 1.8 cm, smooth lobulated margins, well-circumscribed.
- Internal fat attenuation (about -60 HU) and popcorn-pattern calcification.
- Size unchanged across 36 months of CT (1.8 cm to 1.8 cm).
- PET-CT: SUV 1.1, not metabolically active.

## Pathology
Cartilage, adipose tissue and clefts lined by respiratory epithelium. No atypia, no mitoses, no necrosis.

## Final diagnosis and category
Pulmonary hamartoma. Category: BENIGN TUMOR (a true neoplasm, not cancer).

## Plan
No resection. One more CT at 12 months, then discharge from nodule surveillance.

## Why this is NOT lung cancer
Fat plus popcorn calcification, 3-year stability and low PET uptake all point away from malignancy. A smoking history alone does not make a nodule cancer.
