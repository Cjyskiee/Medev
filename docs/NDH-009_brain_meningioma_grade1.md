# Case Report NDH-009: Meningioma, WHO Grade 1 (BENIGN brain tumor that still needed surgery)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-52331 | 63-year-old female | Neurosurgery | 2025-10-01

## Summary
A 3.0 cm brain tumor presenting with a seizure. It is benign by tissue grade, but because it pressed on the brain it was removed. "Benign" does not mean "harmless" or "no treatment".

## Presentation
First-time focal seizure, mild headaches for 6 months.

## Imaging
- MRI: extra-axial, dural-based mass over the right frontal convexity, 3.0 cm, uniform bright enhancement, dural tail, CSF cleft separating it from brain.
- Mild adjacent edema.

## Pathology
Meningothelial meningioma, WHO CNS grade 1. Ki-67 2 percent, no brain invasion, no necrosis, under 4 mitoses per 10 HPF.

## Final diagnosis and category
Meningioma, WHO grade 1. Category: BENIGN TUMOR. Gross total resection (Simpson grade II).

## Plan
Anti-seizure medication. MRI at 3 months, then yearly. No radiation or chemotherapy.

## Why this is NOT glioblastoma or an abscess
Outside the brain tissue, uniform enhancement, low proliferation, no necrosis. Pressure effects are why a benign tumor can be urgent.
