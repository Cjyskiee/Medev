# Case Report NDH-011: Brain Abscess (INFECTION that mimics a malignant tumor)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-50276 | 46-year-old male | Type 2 diabetes, recent dental extraction | Neurosurgery and Infectious Disease | 2026-01-20

## Summary
A 3.2 cm ring-enhancing brain lesion first labelled "probable high-grade glioma or metastasis". Aspiration produced pus. It is an abscess, not a tumor of any kind. Steroids alone and a tumor-style plan would have been dangerous.

## Presentation
Fever 38.4 C, headache for 8 days, mild confusion. Raised white cell count and CRP.

## Imaging
- MRI: left temporal ring-enhancing lesion, 3.2 cm, thin smooth wall that is thinner on the deep side, a small daughter lesion nearby, marked surrounding edema.
- Diffusion: central restricted diffusion (bright DWI, low ADC), the typical pus pattern.

## Microbiology
Aspirated pus grew Streptococcus anginosus. Blood cultures negative.

## Final diagnosis and category
Cerebral abscess. Category: NON-NEOPLASTIC INFECTIOUS MIMIC (not benign tumor, not malignant tumor).

## Plan
Drainage plus 6 weeks of IV ceftriaxone and metronidazole. Dental source treated. Repeat MRI at 2 weeks.

## Why this is NOT glioblastoma
Smooth thin wall, central diffusion restriction, fever, dental source, positive culture. See NDH-010 for the true malignant lookalike.
