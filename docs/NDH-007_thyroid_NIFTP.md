# Case Report NDH-007: NIFTP (thyroid tumor with "papillary-like" nuclei, NOT papillary carcinoma)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-36240 | 38-year-old female | Endocrine Surgery | 2025-05-14

## Summary
A 3.4 cm thyroid nodule with a worrying pathology phrase, "papillary-like nuclear features". The final diagnosis is NIFTP, a low-risk neoplasm that is no longer called cancer. Treating it as papillary thyroid carcinoma would cause overtreatment.

## Presentation
Painless neck swelling. Normal TSH.

## Imaging
- Ultrasound: solid, mildly hypoechoic, well-defined 3.4 cm nodule, smooth margins, no microcalcifications, no suspicious nodes. TI-RADS 4.

## Cytology and molecular
FNA: Bethesda IV (follicular neoplasm). Molecular panel: RAS-type mutation. BRAF V600E negative.

## Pathology
Right lobectomy. Fully encapsulated follicular-patterned tumor with nuclear score 2 to 3 of papillary-like features. No capsular or vascular invasion after full capsule sampling. No true papillae, no necrosis, no mitoses.

## Final diagnosis and category
NIFTP. Category: LOW-RISK NEOPLASM, reclassified as NOT carcinoma (it was called "encapsulated follicular variant of papillary carcinoma" before the 2016 reclassification).

## Plan
Lobectomy is sufficient. No completion thyroidectomy, no radioactive iodine. Annual ultrasound and TSH.

## Why this is NOT papillary thyroid carcinoma
No invasion, RAS-like rather than BRAF-like profile, no nodal disease. The phrase "papillary-like" describes nuclei only and is not a diagnosis of papillary carcinoma. See NDH-008.
