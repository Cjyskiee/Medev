# Case Report NDH-005: Borderline Phyllodes Tumor (BORDERLINE, not benign, not clearly cancer)
Northgate Demo Hospital | SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA, not a real patient, not medical advice
Patient: PT-40688 | 41-year-old female | Breast Surgery | 2025-04-23

## Summary
A breast mass first called "probably fibroadenoma" grew from 3.1 cm to 7.2 cm in 5 months. Excision showed a borderline phyllodes tumor. Treating it like a fibroadenoma (watchful waiting) would have been wrong.

## Presentation
Rapidly enlarging, painless, smooth lump. Skin stretched but intact.

## Imaging
- Ultrasound: lobulated, circumscribed hypoechoic mass with cleft-like spaces. Looked like a fibroadenoma at 3.1 cm.
- Follow-up at 5 months: 7.2 cm.

## Pathology
- Initial core biopsy: "cellular fibroepithelial lesion, favor fibroadenoma". Core samples can miss the diagnostic leaf-like pattern.
- Excision: leaf-like architecture, stromal hypercellularity, moderate atypia, 6 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, focal infiltrative edge. No heterologous elements.

## Final diagnosis and category
Borderline phyllodes tumor (borderline = 5 to 9 mitoses per 10 HPF in this scheme). Category: BORDERLINE TUMOR. It is not benign and not classified as cancer, but it can recur and rarely progress.

## Plan
Re-excision for margin of at least 1 cm. No axillary node surgery. No chemotherapy. Clinical exam and ultrasound every 6 months for 2 years.

## Why this is NOT a fibroadenoma or invasive carcinoma
Rapid growth, leaf-like architecture and mitotic count separate it from NDH-004. It has no invasive ductal cancer cells, unlike NDH-006.
